# Neural Entrainment to Acoustic Edges in Speech

This repository contains the doctoral thesis *Neural Entrainment to Acoustic Edges in Speech*, authored by Oana Cucu and submitted in 2019.

## About the thesis

The thesis develops computational predictive models of stimulus acquisition and encoding and examines the stability of those models. For phoneme encoding, it records an initial hypothesis that the modeled dynamics would be unstable rather than bistable.

The thesis presents the original research idea and analysis in the context of related work. A related paper, “Next-generation neural mass models reproduce features of speech processing,” by Andrew Shannon, David Barton, Martin Homer, and Conor J. Houghton, is described as drawing inspiration from this work. That paper describes a neural-mass model with pitchfork bifurcations associated with harsh and soft sounds.

## Authorship and attribution

Oana Cucu was responsible for the study's experimental design, data collection, data analysis, and thesis writing. Nina Kazanina contributed the overarching conceptual framework. Conor Houghton contributed to the experimental design of the final psychophysics experiment. Both supervisors supervised the research and approved the final data analyses and literature review.

The related paper and its authors are identified above to distinguish that subsequent work from the thesis.
